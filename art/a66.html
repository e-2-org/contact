Conversation between We all peel the onions participants, Paul Danvers (art therapist) and Amy Plant in the art room at the Day Hospital.

Extract 6

E: I was an out-patient and it was different for me, but if you were coming everyday to the day hospital you would have felt that your space had been taken in a way, because they were always in here. But I only came in on the Wednesday afternoons and a Thursday. Originally I think they expected us to be spontaneous and join in which I think was a problem for everyone. But I did sort of get into it. Although after coming in every Wednesday and Thursday, they were just making masks and at one point I wanted to get all these labels and write terms of mental illness on them to title them, because I didn't feel it expressed anything.

So when we went to the exhibition...some of my friends thought it was a primary school exhibition because there was no individuality and Grant's magazine, which to me was...it just didn't match up with the work, it was like two separate things. And also that they had come into a day hospital and there was no expression, I don't feel, about mental illness, it was really about masks...I suppose it was quite symbolic in a way, like putting a mask on...but...so yes, there was disappointment there.

I mean, people felt that they had been used for the project, I didn't feel that, I thought, 'They're artists and they've got to earn a crust', do you know what I mean? And that was it, I didn't mind doing it...I've been coming here for four years and to me it was something different, it was a change and it was only something that lasted so many weeks, and they were OK. There were a few misunderstandings but that could have just been language and culture.

I think it was an experience, it's just a shame that we couldn't do something that matched the magazine. Because Grant was asking all these questions about mental illness and that's bringing up stuff for us, so you're bringing up all this stuff and you're telling him and it's all printed in this magazine and then you've got this exhibition of primary school beads. There was such a huge contrast I can't imagine, people outside, what they would have made of that.

We had to hear how the twins were really vulnerable, they're in a different country, OK fair enough, but they need to realise that we too were vulnerable people coming into a day hospital, people with mental illness...moan, moan, moan, moan!

Amy: What do you think about these two different worlds meeting? Because it brings up all those questions really, should they meet, or should it just be kept separate, or...

E: I don't think it's wrong for them to meet but I think if there was more understanding about it...I just think it could have been a better experience, I don't think they shouldn't meet. But I mean there were nice things. The twins were OK. It was an odd thing to do.
