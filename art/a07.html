Across an Open Space Orla Barry

A public art work on Hampstead Heath
27 July - 26 September 2000

From September 1999, Orla Barry researched Hampstead Heath and the people who use it through the cycle of the seasons. Concentrating on the Heath's public Bathing Ponds and the Lido at Gospel Oak, her research comprised diary entries, notes, overheard snatches of conversation and photographs, plus contributions from people familiar with the Heath. The work resulting from Barry's research comprised signs featuring short pieces of poetic text that appeared in the wild patches on the edges of the ponds, and a free magazine, Refugees, was made in collaboration with users of the Heath and included photographs by Ruth Corney and poetry and drawings by Malvina Cheek. The project was launched at the Parliament Hill Bandstand on July 25 with 'The Green Man', a performer who provided a musical offering, and an Irish ballad singer who performed My Pheasant Lady, written by Orla Barry. However, over the weekend that the signs were installed, a group of Heath users felt the work was inappropriate and reacted to the project by removing and vandalising the signs. No signs now exist and the installation of signs in the Lido had to be cancelled. Refugees was distributed until the end of the summer by volunteers on the Heath.

Orla Barry was born in Ireland and now lives and works in Belgium and Ireland. She studied in Dublin, Belfast, and at De Ateliers, Amsterdam. Solo exhibitions include The Scavenger's Daughter at Shed im Eisenwek, Frauenveld, Switzerland, 1999 and A Barmaids Notebook 1991-98 at Gasworks Gallery, London 1999. Her work has been prominent in numerous group shows including Manifesta, Luxembourg, 1997 and Linguistic Islands, Watou 2000, Belgium, curated by Jan Hoet.
