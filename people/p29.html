Conversation between We all peel the onions participants, Paul Danvers (art therapist) and Amy Plant in the art room at the Day Hospital.

Extract 3

Margaret: It was appalling that people with mental health problems were treated like that. You know the chance for people with mental health problems to gain more confidence. It says about that in here (the transcript of conversations with visitors to the exhibition).

M: I did actually feel quite satisfied with some of the work I did while they were here and I've taken it away and I've got it on my bedroom wall. But that doesn't mean to say that I felt satisfied with the fact that the work was, as I put it earlier, that it was a carnivalisation of our work and our feelings. And for people who are sometimes suffering from enormous emotional pain, that's not the whole picture. It was a skewed view of us and our art. There was a lot more art that if we had the choice, we would have put in. It would have given a much fuller picture of what we're able to produce, as artists and also as people expressing pain. And maybe that's not allowed. Maybe they can't face that.

Amy: It sounds like you were interested in this idea of having an opportunity to have a platform...

M: Yes, very much so.

Amy: ...for the wider public. And I think that a lot of people who came...it was divided, mixed feelings, in a way, from the visitors. On the one hand people were very excited about the materials and the colours and it gave them a joyous feeling and on the other hand they were quite surprised to find out where the work had come from...some people were...and they were trying to look for the pain, if you know what I mean.

K: It's such a specific thing going on here, being in the mental health system that, it seems like, I don't know...it should have more about freedom of expression and showing what was going on in the hospital. I felt that the work produced could have been organised by a school or something like that. I suppose that's what it is, it's making a collection and it's from a psychiatric hospital. I don't know, it was just a trial, a try-out to see what it was like. But a lot of people felt that it interfered with the day-to-day work that they were doing here. Some people did like it...

M: Well, I liked it some of the time, but not all of the time and I certainly didn't like the way it was displayed and organised without any kind of consultation with us. It was as if they were saying to the world, 'Look what we can do, even in a mental hospital, we can make everything jolly, bright and carnival like. Even in a psychiatric hospital, look what we can do, it's so jolly.'
