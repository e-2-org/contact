In reference to Freud's "Interpretation of Dreams" we wanted to depict our own "finding" of an artistic idea. As far as we can remember our inspiration has often happened during the state of waking up or by falling asleep. A condition when the intense thinking of the day, the looking for metaphors, dissolves into oscillation between consciousness and dreaming. It happens, that we come up with a result in the morning, the imagination had engraved it's way while sleeping, the night was working for us. During the day conclusions can be found by an associative way, connecting one thought with another, alertness is needed after the associative linkage of images - to place the work in our own and the artistic context, to refer to a social, sociological "meaning".

We would name our attitude towards art as a "daydream" connecting the aesthetic with ethical contents, an utopic wish dream of a fairer society, of more equality between different individuals .... We have been following this way for years - neither knowing when it will end nor what will be the result of it. There is constant movement, change by referring to incidents in time and space, in connection to other individuals and groups ... private needs and tasks like raising our own kids, interferes with professional life ....

Working in "multiple authorship"

as an attempt to connect different individuals in visual expression. When we work with groups one can compare our position to conductors or film directors - guiding the process and the form of presentation.

When we start a process we have an idea in the back of our head, we try to organise material which is different to what people are used to working with, to make the start more interesting, to invite people to come along with us during the working-period. Here, in the North London Link project, the introduction of sequins was maybe a shock to the people, because they were not used to the material. On the other hand we had the impression, that some participants had been very enthusiastic about the "glittery" stuff. For us it was meant as a way to get in contact with the participants through their interest in the unfamiliar substance. We tell the co-workers what we think of the work, the exhibition space and how the piece could look. It is mostly a quiet, but in spite of this, still exciting beginning. We all have to get into contact with each other. Nobody really knows directly, how the process will develop. Because it depends so much on the people, especially because the handicapped people and the psychiatric patients have their own rhythm and time-schedule. So it is an experiment for the whole group. We think, everybody is curious, as to how it will turn out in the end. Some participants are shy, but some immediately communicate - so a lot functions over verbal and non-verbal conversation. We start to draw, we do work in front of the participants, using the room as a common studio. Maybe they start to take a pencil or paint. Often we sit side by side, we tell about our life, about what it means being an artist in our society, answering questions. It can happen, that participants are telling their whole story of their lives while they are drawing or painting. So this working together has somehow a revealing character.

After some time the working room gets full with drawings and paintings and all this influences the working atmosphere and creates a special energy. There is a big influence of one to another. Maybe one can compare the action to a visual dialogue between participants.

Often the groups we work with, have a naive and uneducated approach towards contemporary art. We try to convey the joy of doing, if one is allowing oneself the freedom of fantasy. We also try to mediate: art is able to cause a strong impact to one's life. It is much more than two hours of art-therapy a week. Making art needs time and energy to get to a result, also to reach this point of satisfaction out of one's own creation. It is a big success for us, if a few people are able to have these experiences, feelings. There are always some participants who are developing a great love towards their own paintings. This is a touching process to watch. But we are never able to motivate all of them. So we are in the position of guiding, observing and trying to help in certain situations.

It can happen, that participants are not able to connect with our way of working, which happened here at the Day Hospital. There was a lot of misunderstanding and misinterpretation of "what we want them to do". Some participants felt forced into a situation, they did not feel invited. But it has also a lot to do with their personalities, with their way of perceiving their world around them, taking in the position of victims. They were used to their safe space in the art therapy room and some felt invaded, intruded by our presence. It was hard for them to deal with change in their daily routine. We did not have the intention to disturb their intimate relationship with their art therapist. If there would not have been any participants liking the process, we would have stopped the project. But, as also Paul Danvers tried to explain it to the group, it was a kind of diversion to what was normally going on. And this brings the risk of failure, of rejection, or it could be perceived as an enrichment of every-day life.

During the whole project the atmosphere was very tense and there was an ambivalence. For us it was very difficult to react to the people, because there was nearly a hostile radiation, which could also quickly change into an enthusiastic, friendly atmosphere. Unfortunately the expectation of some people towards art, their own production, was very high. This can also take away the spontaneous expression and the joy of creation.

So Irene has already brought the text together, and I will also try to respond to all these arguments and quite negative statements from the participants and also the visitors.

As I look at all the photographs lying here in black and white, I have to say I still like the work as a whole. Of course it spreads out joyfulness, of course it does not show so much the pain, the colours are very bright, the pearls and sequins are sparkling in the light, especially when it is getting dark, in the change of daylight.

In a lot of statements there is remembrance of school-kids or nursery work. I think it has more to do with this fear of showing up a kind of nativity in the work, in an art-piece. I have to say, we are not afraid of naivity because it also brings you back to a kind of origin in thoughts or wishes. The demand of creating a masterpiece can also kill your creative-process and is a big trap to fall into, very dangerous to all life expectations. Perhaps it's better to be humble and to learn by doing and working. I have also to discuss the big diversion between the verbal expression and the visual state, as it is also mentioned. People can identify much more with their interviews in Victorya then with their own visual work. I find this a pity, because we spent time together creating something and I have to say, that in between, also big laughs and fun arose. Of course there had been also a kind of worry and sadness in between, and if you look closer you can find it. For example the "lovetrees" of one man, they're showing this incredible loneliness. Others worked more on what they wished to be, a glamorous person, a ballet-dancer, a princess. The people had been very well educated, as we learned from all the conversations. We had a lot of discussions about making masks or not, which was for us just the starting point and not the ongoing theme. We wanted to spread it out, but the motivation was very hard. Some really tried to fight against us without an attempt to get involved. Perhaps it had been the hardest time in a collaboration work. We had different experiences before.

We also asked several times, if the participants wanted to be named, but as it is with group work: some didn't like this idea and it was the majority. So we thought it would be better without naming. In the psychiatric field naming is a sensitive subject anyway, as is showing, the presentation in public. So I wondered that they had been so free in their interviews. Often things are going to be told, but not for the public, it's more a secret you take with you. But the people are used to having intimate conversations, maybe it was similar to a therapeutic setting. I'm glad that all together they were at least satisfied with the title of the show, which arose after a brain-storming afternoon. Unfortunately the participants had been very unsatisfied with the presentation. We know that some wanted to have their own work represented in frames on white gallery walls. Perhaps we made a mistake because we didn't fulfil this wish. But this was of course also a financial question, to make for every different sized piece a frame, which would have been like a vitrine. Because of all the material glued onto the work it became more like a relief. It would have been an unrealistic demand. We knew that. Also the space was hard to work with, with the mamy windows and the structured walls. But we did not mind the situation, in the presentation we reacted to the exhibition facilities. They wanted to show up more their own personality. And because we put everything in one room-piece they can't find themselves anymore. I still can see the individuals. But for a person who just peeps in for a moment, the work becomes one thing. Perhaps this is also a failure, but I'm not sure about it, because it had been a kind of group-process and also working and helping together. For us the harmony in the space is very important, and of course this is then more our interpretation of the work.

We often state, that we're not curators, picking out some work and putting it together in a kind of group-show. It's another approach. We still have the feeling that the spectator is able to get the kind of energy which arose during the process, and we regret that it seems now as we used people or wanted to put them into one direction. I hope we never forced somebody or put the "nose" onto a subject. We believe that it had been a more open process, otherwise we would feel like big monsters.

All we can say now in the end, it was not meant to put three persons work on display, there were more participants involved and it is hard to keep a balance between the single indivdual, so that nobody feels overlooked or disrespected. It still seems as if it did not function this time. We're glad that some people took their work home to put it in their living-rooms, so the project makes sense for us. All these negative arguments and disappointments are also a result of a group-process and it's energy. We want to take more the positive side away from it. In our remembering it is much more the joy of doing something together, the small room getting fuller and fuller of all these colours and sparkling stuff, looking out of the big windows into the green grass of the garden on a grey November afternoon .....

Irene & Christine Hohenbüchler, November 2000
