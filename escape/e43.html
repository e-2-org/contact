Dear Amy,

Part of our research trip to the American deserts this summer brought us to Flagstaff to visit James Turrell's Roden Crater project. While we were in Flagstaff we searched for two days for Adam Chodzko's sign. Unfortunately we did not find it despite our concerted efforts.

On the first day we followed the instructions from the sign standing in the O2 centre. Our first problem was to find the highways, as they were not denoted by number but by destination on the signs. The Leupp road turned out to be the 510 and from there it was easy to find first turning. At this point the measurements became ambiguous; from which point should we measure? There were a number of turnings off the Leupp road but none corresponded to 7 miles from any point. After a few passes we saw that road 244A was marked at a distance closer to 9 miles from the junction. This was a cinder road, which constantly branched out into tracks leading to isolated shacks and houses. To the best of our ability we stayed on the 244A, however it led us in a north-westerly direction onto state property. As we were travelling in an estate car on these rough cinder tracks, and because of our fear of trespassing into a prohibited area we decided to turn back. In the process we sank into the cinders from which we had to push the car free.

That evening we decided not to trust the road numbers and navigate by landmarks all marked on the Ordnance Survey map we picked up at a camping store in Flagstaff. We also decided to hire a 4x4 to make the journey easier.

Driving on the cinder roads was much easier with the truck, and it did not take us long to find our way. We followed the 244A for about half a mile and then turned off in a north-easterly direction towards the Sproul (not Sprout, as spelt in the instructions). This led us to a newly conditioned road. We passed a number of small wells until we reached the one marked on the map. We also passed other noticeable landmarks, such as an alternative community. They were neither marked on the map nor mentioned in the instructions. The location of the well on the map was to the left of the road, however, in reality it lay to the right leaving us uncertain of the accuracy of the instructions. We finally arrived at Roden Springs, which marked the end of the public road going north. A locked gate prevented us following the road to Little Roden Springs. From this point we too could see the Painted Desert and the Roden Crater on the horizon.

Sadly we had to turn back and ended our two-day search for Better Scenery. On the return to the highway we discovered that this conditioned road was the construction access to the Roden Crater.

NB: We have images of the Roden Crater and of Roden Spring. Please contact us if interested.

Desert Fox
