ON THE BEACH

Insouciance is one of those words you save up, waiting for exactly the right time to use it.

Eureka! My first summer in northern climes far removed from London's beloved Heath and there it was - abandoned in sand dunes along with naughty remnants of a tryst - it's colourful covers clashing with the hot golden sand and title 'REFUGEES' screaming out 'Read Me'!

Northumbria, least populated area of England, justifiably boasts miles of stunning beaches punctuated with dunes sporting wild grasses, pretty Tufted Pink Sea Thrift, Red Campion and Sea Asters.

Unique to this area, the Durham Argos butterfly may not be the only unexpected find to the inquisitive exploring adventurer in search of something 'to fill one's void'.

Travel changes your sky but not your mind............ Fast-forwarding through the pages of REFUGEES which, like a good book, provided instant optical instrument to take one away from oneself, sweet memories of invigorating days and steamy nights flooded back - faster than the incoming tide!
Oh to be on Hampstead!

I settled on the sand, shaded by tall marram grass and re-read the contents with more deliberation.

The sweetness of seclusion in such peaceful environs was shattered by a distant nearby giggle.

At 'see level' on my knees, peeping over the dune, I spotted two interacting heavenly bodies part concealed by grass, as snug as two spoons in a silver tray. Spellbound, I stared from a safe, noncommittal distance and was about to do what my conscience dictated when the couple disengaged from friendly assault and rolled apart to reveal 'Adam & Steve' as nature intended. If clothing makes a statement, such masculine nudity left me lost for words. The one with zero crop and tackle to die for smiled on seeing me before returning his attentions to the blonde 6th Former who looked like he had been reared on wild strawberries, asparagus tips, perrier water and diet dental floss! For one devoted to a religion of Eros, a devotee of Bacchus and professor of desire, here was quality eye candy - the original 'bubble-butt boy'!

A frisson of expectation wafted across the dunes like the gentle sea breeze, heralding my surprise discovery that I had chanced upon Blyth's answer to Hampstead heath or the Pere-Lachaise cemetery in Paris (where Oscar Wilde.... 'the only way to get rid of temptation is to yield to it'.......rests in his heaven).

In later conversation with another chance encounter and the assistance of my filed glasses, my findings were confirmed! - Not only Ross Gulls (that follow fishing boats from the Arctic back to the Tyne), majestic Fulmars or Puffins, but much, much more for the beach-wise adventurer to see and do.

..............a bit like the Heath, minus dendrophiles!

On my next visit to this sandy oasis away from the sounds and sights of the inhabited world, I shall assume the station of an outcast REFUGEE, devoted beyond the confines of the other world in this lonely and enchanting penance.

Who knows? I may even get picked up!

Tom Graham    August 2000
