Mecca Anna Best

The State Mecca Bingo Hall, Kilburn, London, NW6
1-30 June 1999

Between October 1998 and June 1999, Anna Best worked at the State Mecca Bingo Hall in Kilburn, once one of Europe's largest cinemas and a concert venue, researching connections between gambling and religion based on a series of conversations with artists, academics, dedicated 'believers', bingo players and staff at Mecca, about connections between gambling and religion, addictions and superstition, luck, hopes, dreams and miracles. The resulting images, film clips, lucky charms and numbers were projected like apparitions around the building, one image for each day, throughout the month of June. A special bingo game took place, called by artist Karen Stripp, featuring a duet between the Random Number Generator and the Wurlitzer organ, with music assembled and played by composer Paul Whitty.

A website, www.eyes-down.com edited by Anna Best and Karen Stripp, is the third part of Mecca. It archives and highlights the various elements of the project which took place at Camden Arts Centre and the State Mecca Bingo Hall and can be found online at www.eyes-down.com.

Anna Best is based in London, and has made and exhibited work extensively in Britain and abroad, including projects, exhibitions and collaborations at Grizedale, Ambleside, Cumbria, 2000; Artsway, New Forest, 2000; The Liverpool Biennial, 1999, and The Wedding Project with Tate Modern, 1998.

Supported by members of staff at The State Mecca Bingo Hall, Kilburn, insight lighting, Loot Newspaper, the Rank Group and the Ronald Grant Archive.
