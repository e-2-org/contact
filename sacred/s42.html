Conversation between We all peel the onions participants, Paul Danvers (art therapist) and Amy Plant in the art room at the Day Hospital.

Extract 5

M: It shows that people didn't know what they were looking at anyway because people thought it was part of an art therapy class, but it's very far from what we do in art therapy, it's a million miles away from what we do in art therapy. No wonder they were surprised because what we do in art therapy is much more likely to show the pain and the torment.

Amy: I think it should have been made clearer that it was in the context of an art therapy group...

Margaret: No it wasn't.

M: You see, even now, even now.

Margaret: For the first two weeks art therapy went by the board.

Paul: It was quite outside of the art therapy. That was quite clear. In fact what the Hohenbüchlers said, they made it quite clear that what they were doing definitely wasn't art therapy, but they also said that, 'What we do goes beyond therapy'.

M: That's a bit cheeky isn't it?

Paul: I thought that it sounded exciting, beyond therapy. And it was for a public space, so this is a complete antithesis to what we normally do. There was a big difference in direction.

M: I agree, in therapy people are close together and there's a trust, a bond of trust, there's terrific sensitivity so people are aware of each other's feelings.

Amy: So when you were making the work what did you feel at the time that you were getting out of it?

Margaret: I felt resentful and irritated nearly all the time and torn between not joining in like A and J, being on the outside and not wanting to be on the outside. I wanted to join in what seemed to be...people seemed to be enjoying themselves...

M: Oh, I was enthusiastic, quite enthusiastic about using some of the materials some of the time. It was fun to be able to use sequins, not so much the beads. But I didn't want that to be the only thing I did.

Margaret: Well, I felt torn between the whole thing and then I had a breakdown towards the end, I got really bad actually. They were quite kind to me then. Most of the time I felt torn between joining in and staying out.

Amy: If they had introduced something which was even more removed from what normally happens in this room, for instance if it had been a video project which would have happened one day a week or something like that, do you feel that would have been more appropriate? If it was something really far away, really different, do think that would have made a difference?

Margaret: I think if it had been one day a week...I actually thought it was going to be something totally different, I was looking forward to trying something new.

C: I was here and I thought it was excellent. I liked all the materials, something different, and I suppose from my point of view that I wasn't pressurised into doing anything. I did enjoy everything.

Amy: So were you involved in the project at all?

N: No I wasn't. Not in one single way. I didn't stick a single sequin on a single piece of paper.

Paul: But you were here.

N: I wasn't here, I was in Thailand for three months, how could I have been here?

C: How did you know they had sequins then?

N: Well, you can't really avoid the sequins. No, I saw it sort of start off and then I went away for three months and I came back and it was all over.

C: Did you go away because of the project?

N: I'd like to say yes, but I just went away for my own sanity. But I wasn't... I didn't move any tables, dismantle any walls....

Paul: But you did comment.

N: Yes, but that's because I'm a manic depressive, and I can't shut up sometimes. I was here when they arrived and I was very unresponsive not due to anything...I was going away, I'd booked my air ticket.

Amy: So it wasn't worth you getting involved in something new.

N: Worth, I don't know. No, I probably wouldn't have got involved at that stage. I probably wouldn't have come to art as much as I'd like to. But who knows? That's in another universe, a parallel dimension.
